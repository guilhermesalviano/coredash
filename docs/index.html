Service registry

Every widget is a service you can turn on or off.

CoreDash ships as a grid of independent cards. Wire up the APIs you care about; the rest stay dark. Here's the full roster running on the reference build.

01 WEATHER Live conditions, feels-like, and an hourly strip — flags rain before you walk out the door. online
02 CALENDAR Counts down to the birthdays, trips, and deadlines you told it mattered. online
03 STOCKS Ticker-by-ticker price and change on every load, pulled straight from Yahoo Finance. online
04 HABITS One tap a day, a week view, and a streak counter that isn't shy about breaking it. online
05 TODO Fast add, fast check-off, a progress bar for the day — nothing to configure. online
06 GOALS The longer-range targets that sit above your daily list, always in view. online
07 GMAIL Unread previews and one-click mark-as-read, without opening another tab. online
08 SPOTIFY Search, queue, library, and device switching — full transport control on-screen. online
09 NEWS Curated headlines with a blur toggle, for when you're not ready for spoilers yet. online
10 WISHLIST & PRODUCTS Watches your Amazon wishlist and any product URL you add, and surfaces price drops. online
11 FLIGHTS Tracks fares on the routes you save and flags when one is worth booking. online
12 AI NARRATIVE A short daily brief written from your own data, generated locally via Ollama or Gemini. online
13 ALERTS One feed for every price drop, fare drop, and stock target the other widgets catch. online
Design goals

Built for a spare tablet, not a server room.

CoreDash targets the kind of hardware already gathering dust in a drawer — an old Pi, a thin client, a NAS that's mostly idle.

low-cost hardware

Runs on what you already own

No GPU, no rack. A Raspberry Pi or an old mini-PC on your home network is enough to keep it running 24/7.

your data, your box

Nothing leaves your network

Credentials and API keys live in your own .env file. There's no CoreDash server collecting your habits or your inbox.

open source

Fork it, extend it

Every widget is a plain Next.js card. Add your own API route and drop a new one into the grid.

Getting started

Up in about the time it takes to read this.

Pull the image, point it at port 3000, and start filling in your API keys. No build step required to try it.

~/homelab 
$ docker run -d \
  -p 3000:3000 \
  --name coredash \
  guilhermesalviano/coredash:latest
# CoreDash is now listening on :3000
# docker-compose.yml
services:
  coredash:
    image: guilhermesalviano/coredash:latest
    ports:
      - "3000:3000"
    restart: unless-stopped

$ docker compose up -d
  1. 01 Point a browser (or your wall tablet) at http://<host-ip>:3000.
  2. 02 Copy .env.example to .env and add the API keys for the widgets you want live.
  3. 03 Leave the rest unset — unconfigured widgets simply stay off the grid.
